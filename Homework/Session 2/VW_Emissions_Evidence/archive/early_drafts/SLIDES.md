## Slide 1 — An independent study, not a regulator, found the first gap

**Headline:** In 2014, a university research group — not the EPA, not VW —
found something regulators' own certification tests had missed.

- West Virginia University (WVU), commissioned by the International Council on
  Clean Transportation (ICCT), road-tested three diesel cars with portable
  emissions equipment (PEMS) — the goal was to *promote* clean diesel, not to
  investigate anyone.
- Three vehicles, five real driving routes around California, each vehicle
  also run through the official lab certification cycle for comparison.
- Published May 2014. No accusation of wrongdoing — just a measurement result
  that didn't add up.

Slide 2's reveal.)*

## Slide 2 — The chart: lab-legal, road-illegal

**Headline:** The same cars that passed the lab test emitted 5–35× the legal
NOx limit on the road.

**[Insert `chart/nox_chart.png`]**

- Legal standard: **0.043 g/km NOx** (US-EPA Tier2-Bin5, full useful life).
- On the certification cycle (FTP-75), both tested vehicles measured *below*
  the standard: 0.022 g/km and 0.016 g/km.
- On the road, across five different driving conditions: Vehicle A (LNT)
  measured **15–35×** the standard; Vehicle B (urea-SCR) measured **5–20×**.
- Vehicle C, a third car with a different SCR implementation, stayed at or
  near the standard almost everywhere — proof this wasn't just "diesel cars
  are dirty on the road," since a same-technology, different-vehicle
  comparison (B vs. C) shows a real difference between individual vehicles.

*(These are exceedance-of-the-legal-standard multiples on different road
routes — not a road-to-lab ratio. Say this explicitly if asked.)*

## Slide 3 — Why a passed test and a failed real-world result can both be true

**Headline:** A certification measurement can be completely accurate under its
own test conditions and still fail to represent ordinary driving.

**[Insert `chart/lab_vs_road_diagram.png`]**

- The lab cycle is a fixed, published, repeatable speed trace. Real-world
  driving is not — different speed, load, grade, and traffic every time.
- WVU's finding, on its own, only established a **discrepancy** — the report
  speculates about possible mechanical causes (filter regeneration cycles,
  catalyst sizing) without concluding intent.
- **What the study did *not* claim:** that this was deliberate, that it
  applied to VW's whole fleet, or what specific vehicles were tested (WVU
  anonymized them as "Vehicle A/B/C" on purpose).
- That's the handoff to [next section]: EPA, CARB, and the DOJ's later
  investigation is what established *why* the gap existed — a defeat device
  that detected the test pattern and switched modes.
